\begin{abstract}
Recent work proving that the matrix-multiplication exponent satisfies
$\omega \leq 9/4$ motivates the search for explicit finite algorithms
with improved recursive complexity. We present
\texttt{fast\_mm\_search}, a numerical-to-exact procedure that searches
for bilinear matrix-multiplication decompositions from random
coefficients, reconstructs exact identities, and verifies them using
exact arithmetic. As a controlled validation, we specified only the
$2\times2$, rank-7 problem and withheld Strassen's known construction.
The search recovered an exact seven-product algorithm with integer
coefficients and a representation different from the standard
Strassen formula. Exact rational verification established equality
for all 64 coefficients of the multiplication tensor. This blind
recovery demonstrates that exact finite matrix-multiplication
algorithms can be recovered automatically and provides a validated
procedure for searching finite $(u,r)$ decompositions whose explicit
solutions are unknown.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

Strassen showed that $2\times2$ matrix multiplication can be computed
with seven bilinear products instead of the eight used by the direct
algorithm \cite{strassen1969}. Recursive application gives arithmetic
exponent
\[
\log_2 7 \simeq 2.80735.
\]

Recent work proves the stronger asymptotic bound
\[
\omega \leq \frac{9}{4}
\]
over $\mathbb{C}$ \cite{openai2026}. This motivates the constructive
search for explicit finite matrix-multiplication decompositions with
improved recursive complexity.

We developed \texttt{fast\_mm\_search} to search for such
decompositions numerically, reconstruct candidate coefficients
exactly, and verify the resulting identities independently. The
$2\times2$, rank-7 problem provides a controlled validation because
an exact solution is known while its coefficients are withheld from
the search.

\section{Methods}

Let
\[
T \in \mathbb{Q}^{4\times4\times4}
\]
denote the tensor for $2\times2$ matrix multiplication. A rank-$r$
bilinear algorithm has the decomposition
\[
\widehat{T}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{r}
u_k \otimes v_k \otimes w_k,
\]
where $u_k$ and $v_k$ define linear forms in the entries of the two
input matrices and $w_k$ distributes each bilinear product among the
output entries.

We fixed $r=7$ and optimised the coefficients from independent random
initial values using L-BFGS-B. Candidates were ranked by relative
tensor residual
\[
\rho
=
\frac{\lVert T-\widehat{T}\rVert_F}
     {\lVert T\rVert_F}.
\]

Candidates reaching the numerical threshold were term-normalised,
reconstructed as rational coefficients, and passed to a separate
verifier using exact rational arithmetic. Numerical optimisation
selects candidates; exact tensor equality determines acceptance.

\section{Results}

The campaign evaluated 581 independent random initialisations. The
lowest numerical residual,
\[
1.596061\times10^{-8},
\]
occurred at seed 482 and did not yield an exact verified identity.
Seed 581 converged after 140 L-BFGS-B iterations to
\[
\rho = 3.093299\times10^{-8}.
\]
Exact reconstruction produced coefficients with denominator one.

For
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
a & b\\
c & d
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=
\begin{pmatrix}
e & f\\
g & h
\end{pmatrix},
\]
the recovered algorithm computes
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_1 &= (b-d)h, &
P_2 &= a(e+f+g+h),\\
P_3 &= (a-b)(g+h), &
P_4 &= (a-c)f,\\
P_5 &= (c-d)e, &
P_6 &= d(e+g),\\
P_7 &= (a-d)(e+g+h),
\end{aligned}
\]
and reconstructs
\[
AB=
\begin{pmatrix}
-P_1-P_3+P_6+P_7 &
P_1+P_2-P_6-P_7\\
P_5+P_6 &
P_2-P_4-P_6-P_7
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Expansion gives
\[
AB=
\begin{pmatrix}
ae+bg & af+bh\\
ce+dg & cf+dh
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Exact verification evaluated all
\[
4\times4\times4=64
\]
coefficients of the multiplication tensor. All coefficients matched,
giving zero mismatches and maximum exact residual zero.

\section{Discussion}

The recovered identity attains the classical rank-7 bound for
$2\times2$ matrix multiplication. Its coefficient representation
differs from the standard presentation of Strassen's construction,
and it was recovered from random initial coefficients without access
to Strassen's formula.

The campaign also separates numerical approximation from mathematical
verification. Seed 482 attained the lowest floating-point residual,
while seed 581 produced the first exactly reconstructable identity.
The numerical objective alone therefore does not determine whether a
candidate admits the exact representation required for proof.

This experiment validates the numerical search, exact reconstruction,
and independent verification pipeline. The same procedure can now be
applied to finite $(u,r)$ problems whose explicit decompositions are
unknown.
